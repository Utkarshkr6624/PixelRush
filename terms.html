The short version

PixelRush is a free browser arcade. It runs on plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript in your tab. There is no account, no subscription, no in-game purchase and nothing to cancel. The site carries advertising, which is the only reason it exists. In exchange, here is the deal in seven lines:

  • The games are free, provided as they are, with no warranty of any kind.
  • Play them for yourself. Personal and non-commercial use only.
  • Ads may be shown alongside the games. They are never chosen or endorsed by us, and blocking them is entirely your decision.
  • Games can be changed, balanced or switched off whenever the arcade decides.
  • Link to the site and share links freely. Do not republish the games themselves.
  • No account means your high scores live in your own browser and are nobody else's problem when they go.
  • Do not scrape the site, overload it with automation, or try to break the games. To your advantage or anyone else's.

The rest of this page unpacks each of those. Read it once, then forget about it and go and beat your own score.

Play it for yourself: personal, non-commercial use

The games on PixelRush are for your own enjoyment. That means playing them yourself, on your own device, for your own amusement. It also means you may not use them, or the site, to make money or to run somebody else's business.

Concretely, the following are not permitted without asking us first:

  • Selling access. Charging people to play, or bundling the games into a paid product, a subscription service, a paywalled newsletter or a membership.
  • Rebranding and reselling. Putting the games on your own site or app under your own name, with or without changes, for money or for traffic.
  • Advertising against the site. Running ad campaigns, pop-unders or ad networks that buy traffic to these pages and intercept the click.
  • Commercial data work. Harvesting scores, player behaviour or site telemetry to feed someone else's product, model or trading strategy.
  • Embedding the games in a kiosk, stream overlay, classroom or venue product where people watch or pay to watch other people play.

Playing on a shared computer, on a work machine, on a school lab PC or on a library terminal is fine. So is letting a friend play the next round on your laptop. The line is at whether the games have become your product rather than your pastime.

The site and the games can change at any time

PixelRush is a living arcade, not a frozen release. Games get balance changes. Controls move. Difficulty shifts, new games land, and occasionally an old cabinet is quietly retired because it is not as much fun as it used to be. We do this without asking permission and without warning, and we may do it at any time, including removing a game entirely.

That means a link you bookmarked to a specific game may stop working one morning, a shortcut on your home screen may land somewhere else, and a page you wrote a guide about may describe rules that have since changed. We are not obliged to keep a deprecated game alive for your convenience. Where we can do it cheaply, we will leave a page explaining that a game has moved on rather than deleting it silently.

We may also change or remove any part of the site itself — the design, the navigation, the filters, the ticker, the layout. Do not build anything that depends on the current shape of these pages.

High scores live in your browser, not on a server

Because there is no account and no server, your scores and settings are stored locally in your own browser. That part of the privacy story is a good one: we never see your scores, and we cannot see them from here. It does not cover everything the page does, though — the advertising below is a separate business.

It also means the scores are entirely your problem to look after. They are attached to one browser on one device, and they are erased when that browser data is erased. You will lose them if you:

  • clear your browsing data, cookies or site data, or use “clear cookies on exit”;
  • play in a private or incognito window, which forgets everything the moment you close it;
  • switch browser, device or operating system;
  • use a cleaning tool, a private browsing extension or a shared kiosk PC;
  • delete the game's local data from your browser's storage settings.

We cannot recover them for you. There is no backup, because there is nowhere for a backup to live. If a score matters to you, write it down. Equally, no one else can take it from you: your scores are not submitted anywhere, not uploaded anywhere, and not shared with anyone — including us.

Advertising

The arcade is funded by advertising. Pages here may show ads, and ads may appear before a game loads or around the play area. There is no paywall, no trial timer, no “remove ads” button and no premium tier. Every game stays free to open and free to finish, and no ad will ever sit between you and a game you have started.

The ads are chosen and served by third parties, not by us. We do not select which ads appear, we do not endorse them, and we do not control what they say or where they send you. We have not chosen a specific ad provider, so rather than name one, here is the honest description of the category: advertising on a site like this is normally delivered by a third-party ad network, which can set cookies or similar storage in your browser, read the IP address your request arrives from, and use that to build a profile and decide which ad to show you. When a provider is chosen, its name and its own privacy policy will be listed here and on the privacy policy page.

We do not receive the profiling data they build, and we cannot see it, correct it or delete it. If you would rather not be profiled, the opt-out lives in your own browser and privacy settings and in the ad provider’s own controls, not with us. Blocking ads is your call entirely: use a content blocker, your browser’s tracking protection, or your operating system’s setting. Please note that a blocker is a tool on your machine, not a promise from us.

If an ad is offensive, misleading, broken, or sits over a game and stops you playing, tell us at Utkarsh6624@gmail.com with “Advertising” in the subject line and a note of which page it happened on. We will pass the complaint to the provider where we can, but the ad is theirs, so the outcome is theirs too.
