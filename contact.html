Pick your route

Four ways in, one inbox

Whichever route you take, it is the same address and the same reader. The only thing that changes is the subject line — and getting that right is what stops your message getting filed in the wrong pile. Each route below comes with a ready-made button that fills the subject in for you, so you can just start typing.

1. Suggest a game

You played something on another site and thought we should have it. Or you invented a mechanic in the shower and want to know whether it would fit here. Both are worth sending. Ideas that are already in the pipeline get an honest answer, even when it is no.

Suggested subject line:

Game suggestion: <the name>

Include, if you can:

  • What it is. One line on the core mechanic. A bullet list of rules is fine.
  • Why it belongs here. The genre it would sit in, and roughly how long one run takes.
  • Where you played it. A link or the name of the app, if it exists somewhere already.
  • Controls. Keyboard, one-thumb on mobile, or both. This genuinely changes the build.
Suggest a game

2. Report a bug on a specific game

Something broke, or something never worked in the first place. This is the route that actually gets things fixed, so be as specific as you can and name the game in the subject line. "The snake one" is not enough — we ship more than one of everything eventually.

Suggested subject line:

Bug: <game name> on <browser>

An example of a good one:

Bug: Neon Snake on Safari (iPhone 15, iOS 18)

Include, if you can:

  • Which game. The name shown on the card, not a description of it.
  • Browser and version. Chrome, Firefox, Safari or Edge — and the version number if the browser is showing one.
  • Device. Desktop, tablet or phone, plus the operating system.
  • What you did. The exact inputs, in order. "Held left for about three seconds."
  • What you expected, and what happened instead, including any message on screen.
  • How often. Every single run, or once in ten. That decides how fast it gets looked at.

3. General feedback, or just saying hello

Difficulty feels off. The colours are hard to read on a dim screen. You want more of a genre you already like. You just wanted to say the site is good. All of that is welcome and all of it gets read, even when the answer is "noted, and here is why we did it this way".

Suggested subject line:

Feedback: <the thing you want to talk about>

A plain Hello is fine too. Include the game and device if your point is about how something plays — that context is the part that makes feedback actionable. If you are reporting a fault, use route 2 instead so it does not get lost in general mail.

4. A grievance, a formal complaint, a takedown request or a legal notice

That is the fourth route. The three above it are everyday mail. This one is for anything that has to be on the record: a complaint about the site or the way it is run, a request to have something taken down, a legal notice, or an order from a court or a platform. Same inbox as the rest, same reader — but the subject line starts a clock, and an ordinary email does not.

Not for bugs. A cabinet that will not boot belongs in route 2, where it gets fixed. Sending it here instead just means the real grievances wait behind it.

Suggested subject line:

Grievance: <what it is about>

Include, if you can:

  • What you want. A reply, a removal, a fix, or a change to how the site is run. Say which one.
  • Where it is. The page or the game, and the exact words, image or sound you are objecting to.
  • Why it is wrong. Plain words are fine. You do not have to quote a section of anything.
  • Whether you want a reply. Some notices want an acknowledgement and nothing else.

Bug reports only

Before you write about a bug

A minute of checking saves both of us a thread. None of this is a fob — if you have already done it, send the report anyway. It is just the fastest route to an actual fix.

  1. Reload the page. Every cabinet is a fresh boot. If the fault is still there afterwards, it is a real one.
  2. Try a private window. A saved high score or a stuck keypress in this tab can make a game look broken when it is fine.
  3. Restart the run. Press R, or tap restart, and reproduce the exact moment again. Half of all faults only appear on the second run.
  4. Check whether the motion toggle is the problem. The ON/OFF button in the header disables the parallax and reveal effects. If the game only misbehaves with it on, say so in the report — that is a different bug to the one I would otherwise go hunting for.
  5. Test on one other browser. If it works in Firefox and dies in Chrome, say both. If it dies everywhere, say that too. It tells me where to start looking.
  6. Note whether it blocks you. A cosmetic glitch and a one-button-is-dead freeze get looked at in a different order. Saying which one you hit is not complaining — it is routing.

Then send it. The address is Utkarsh6624@gmail.com with the game and your browser in the subject line.
