PRIVACY POLICY

This is the whole thing, in plain English. PixelRush still has no account and no database, and your high score still lives in your own browser. The arcade now carries advertising, so a third party is involved — and this page says exactly what that means, what it does not mean, and how you switch it off.

PixelRush privacy summary: no accounts, no sign-up, no analytics scripts run by us, and no sale of personal data. The page carries advertising served by a third party, which may set cookies or similar storage, see your IP address and build a profile; we do not receive that data. High scores are stored only in your own browser. The page goes on to cover your rights under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, what happens with children's data, who runs the site and how to reach them in writing, how to raise a grievance with the named grievance officer, and how to request a translation of this policy.

The short version

PixelRush is a folder of static files. It has no back end, no database, no user table and no admin panel. We cannot see you, we do not want to, and there is no button anywhere on the site that would let us start. The one new thing is advertising, and that comes with a company we do not control — so here is the line between the two.

  • No sign-up, ever. There is no account system, so there is no account to profile and nothing you do is gated behind one.
  • No analytics run by us. No Google Analytics, no Plausible, no counters, no heat maps on our side of the page.
  • Advertising is served by someone else. The arcade carries ads. A third-party ad company may set cookies or similar storage, may see your IP address, and may build a profile from it. We do not control that and we do not get a copy of it. It is written up properly in “Advertising” below.
  • No cookies set by us. Not one. Not even a “did you enjoy this page?” one. If a cookie turns up on this site, it came from the ad company or the font host, not from PixelRush.
  • Your score stays put. High scores live in your own browser’s local storage, on your device only.
  • Still free, still no paywall. No trial timer, no “remove ads” button, no premium tier, no in-app purchases. Ads pay the hosting; they never charge you for a game.

No accounts and no sign-up

There is no register button, no login form, no “connect your Steam account” and no newsletter box. The arcade is open from the first second. If a future version of the site ever introduces an account or a mailing list, this page will be updated before that goes live, and it will say exactly what would be stored and why.

What we run, and what the ad company runs

The site loads exactly two scripts of our own: js/engine.js and js/site.js. Both are first-party files sitting in this site's own js/ folder. Read them — they are a few hundred lines of vanilla JavaScript that move particles, wire up the menu and reveal text on scroll. Neither one makes a network request, opens a socket, reads a device identifier or writes anything to a remote service.

On our side of the page there is no Google Analytics, no tag manager, no A/B testing tool, no session recorder, no heat-map tool, no chat widget, no social share buttons and no embedded third-party content. We do not fingerprint your browser, we do not canvas-hash it, and we do not use a “unique visitor” counter dressed up as a statistic. The counters on the home page are fixed numbers written into the page. They are not read off a database, because there is no database.

What we do not run is not the same as what appears on the page. The ad company’s code is there, it is not ours, and it is described properly in the next section rather than glossed over here.

We do not sell, rent, trade or share personal data, because we hold no personal data to sell. We also do not receive the ad company’s data, so there is nothing of yours in our hands to pass on. If that ever changes, it will be written here first.

Advertising

The arcade now carries advertising. That is the deal: the games are free, there is no paywall and no subscription, and the ads are what keeps the hosting bill paid. It also means a company we do not control is involved in your visit, so here is exactly how that works.

  • Who serves the ads. A third-party advertising company, not PixelRush. The provider has not been chosen yet, so this page names the category rather than a company. When one is picked, its name, its privacy policy and its opt-out page will be listed here, in this section, before the ads go live.
  • What they can see. Serving an ad means their systems see your IP address, and they will usually also see your browser, your device, your screen size and your approximate location, because that is what an IP address roughly reveals.
  • What they can store. They can set cookies, and they can use similar storage such as local storage or an advertising identifier. Pixels and beacons — the invisible requests used to check whether an ad was shown, and whether you clicked it — are part of the same mechanism.
  • What they can build. They can use all of that to work out what you might like, and to profile you for advertising purposes across sites. That is a profile about your browsing, not about your account, because you do not have one.
  • What we get. Nothing about you. The ad company does not hand us your profile, your IP address or its identifiers, and we cannot look them up. We can see aggregate figures such as how many times an ad was shown, and we cannot tell which visitor that was.
  • What this does not touch. Your high scores, your game settings and your motion toggle. Those stay in your browser’s local storage, on your device, and are never sent to us or to the ad company.

If you would rather not be measured, you have three levers, in the order most people like them:

  • Your own controls. Every major browser has a tracking-protection or “privacy” setting that limits third-party cookies and cross-site tracking. Turning it on is your decision, and it costs this site nothing.
  • The ad company’s controls. The provider will offer its own opt-out and “your privacy choices” page, and once a provider is named that page will be linked from here. That is where you ask them to stop profiling you.
  • A blocker. A content blocker or a privacy extension will hide the ads, and with them the storage. Every game still plays, because no game is bought, timed or unlocked with an ad.

We are not going to describe this site as free of tracking, because an ad is a tracking mechanism whether or not anyone says the word. What we can say is narrower and true: there is no account, no profile on our side, and nothing here is for sale.

Cookies and storage

PixelRush sets no cookies. Not a session cookie, not a preference cookie, not a “we would love to know if you came from a search” cookie. Nothing is stored by us on your device through cookies, and nothing is read back from one.

Third parties on the page do use them. The ad company sets its own cookies and similar storage, and the font host can see the request that fetches a typeface. You can tell them apart in your browser’s site-data list by who set them. If you follow a link to another website, that website may set its own cookies too — that is between you and them, and this policy stops at our front door.

High scores and settings live in your browser

Some games save a personal best, and the site remembers a few preferences, such as whether you have switched motion effects off. All of it is written to localStorage — a storage area built into your own browser. The data is written to your device, read back by the same page on the same device, and never transmitted anywhere. It is a note your browser passes to itself, not a message it sends to us.

The practical consequences are worth spelling out, because they cut both ways:

  • Your scores do not sync between your phone and your laptop. There is no account to sync through.
  • Clearing your browser data wipes your scores. That is the trade this site makes on purpose — your scores leave no trace on us at all.
  • Private or incognito windows usually throw the data away the moment you close the tab.
  • Browsing with cookies and site data blocked, or with storage restricted, will simply mean scores do not stick between visits. Every game still plays.

How to clear what is stored

Nothing we store is sensitive, and clearing it takes a few seconds. Pick whichever of these suits you:

  • All of it, in one go. Open your browser settings, find Clear browsing data (Chrome, Edge and Firefox all call it roughly that), tick cached files and cookies, and clear. Your PixelRush scores and settings go with it.
  • Just this site's data. In Chrome or Edge, click the padlock or the tune icon in the address bar, then Cookies and site data → Manage on-device site data → delete anything for the arcade's domain.
  • Just the arcade's storage. Open the developer console on any PixelRush page and run localStorage.clear(), then reload.
  • One game at a time. Each cabinet keeps its own entry under a namespaced key, so clearing a single game's entry leaves the rest alone.
  • Ad storage set by others. The steps above clear what the site stored, and the first two also clear the cookies the ad company has set, because those are site data too. The ad company’s own profile of you lives on their side, so ask them to delete it using the opt-out link on their privacy page.

Your browser may also offer a tracking protection setting such as “Do Not Track” or “Strict privacy”. Leave it on if you like. It costs this site nothing, and it limits at least some of what the ad company can do.

Web fonts, and what Google can see

The arcade uses two typefaces, and loading them is the second place where a third party is genuinely involved. The font files are served by Google Fonts from fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com, rather than being bundled with the site.

When your browser fetches a font, that request necessarily carries your IP address, along with the usual request headers. That means Google can see that your IP requested a font, and when. We cannot prevent that without self-hosting the font files, and we are telling you rather than quietly hoping you do not notice. We do not send Google any identifiers of our own, we run no Google analytics or Google advertising product on this site, and no Google account is required to load a web font.

If you would rather Google saw nothing at all, the standard defences all work here too: a content blocker, a hardened privacy extension, or your browser's built-in tracking protection. The pages fall back to system fonts and stay perfectly readable.

How the site is served

PixelRush is a set of static files — HTML, CSS, JavaScript and images — sitting on a host that does nothing but hand them over. There is no application server, no server-side language, no database, no user table and no login service. When you open a page, the server's whole job is to send you that file.

Whoever hosts the files keeps ordinary web server logs, because keeping them is how every host on the internet stays alive: the requests made, the time, the file returned, the status code and the IP address they came from. Those logs are used for security, abuse handling and keeping the site running, and they are typically rotated or deleted within weeks. We do not read them to work out who you are, we do not use them to build a profile, and we do not add anything to them.

Your rights under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 gives an individual a set of rights over their own personal data. In ordinary words, you can ask a service what it holds about you, ask for anything wrong to be corrected, ask for it to be deleted, change your mind about something you agreed to, and complain if you think it is all being handled badly.

  • Know what is held. Ask an organisation for a copy of the personal data it has about you.
  • Get it corrected. Ask for anything inaccurate to be fixed.
  • Get it erased. Ask for data to be deleted once the reason for holding it has gone.
  • Withdraw consent. Change your mind about something you agreed to, without having to argue for it.
  • Complain upwards. If a service ignores your request, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India, once it is constituted and operating.

Now the part that matters on this site. PixelRush has no accounts and no database. There is no file on our side with your name on it, so in most cases there is genuinely nothing for us to disclose, correct or erase.

There is one exception, and it is one you choose: an email. If you write to us, we hold what you put in the message and your email address, and nothing else. Ask us to delete it at any time and we will, without asking you to justify it.

The advertising profile written up in “Advertising” above is a separate thing, and it is worth being exact about it. That profile belongs to the advertising company, not to PixelRush. We never receive it and we could not hand it over, because we never had it. Your rights over it are exercised through that company's own privacy notice and opt-out page, not with us. That page is linked from “Advertising” above as soon as a provider is named.

If a data question we can actually answer has not been answered to your liking, send it to the grievance officer below rather than to the general mailbox, so it goes on the thirty-day clock instead of waiting for the next time we happen to read a subject line.

Children

Players of all ages are welcome, including children — there is no age gate, no sign-up wall and nothing to sign up for, and nothing we collect is tied to a person, because there is no person to attach it to. We gather no data from anyone under 18 ourselves.

The advertising does not know who you are either — no ad company here is given a child’s name, an email address or a date of birth — but it can still see an IP address and set storage, and most ad providers exclude known child users on request rather than by default. If you want a clean slate, a guardian can use the clearing steps above and get back to a blank page in under a minute. If you want the ads gone entirely, the browser or blocker options in “Advertising” are the way.

The IT Rules 2021 ask for extra care with data about children. Among other things they say a service should not track children, or run behavioural advertising aimed at them, without verifiable consent from a parent or guardian first.

PixelRush does none of those things. There is no registration for anyone, no behavioural advertising aimed at children, and no data at all collected from a person under 18. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children, and because we do not collect personal data from anyone, there is no profile of a child sitting on our side for anyone to worry about.

If you are a parent or guardian with a specific concern about your child and this site, or you just want the browser data gone from a shared family machine, use the clearing steps above — they take under a minute — or email us and you will get an answer from the same person who wrote this page.
